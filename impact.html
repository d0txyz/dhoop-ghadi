Would the advice have held up in a real summer?

What was tested

  1. For every day from to , in places, the agent planned the day from the weather forecast as it was issued the day before. That is what a worker would have seen on their phone.
  2. That plan was then scored against the weather that actually happened on the day.
  3. A fixed 9 am to 5 pm shift was scored against the same actual weather.

The planner and the heat index in this test are the same files the app runs. Anyone can repeat it: node backtest/run.js.

What the words mean

  • Severe-heat hour: an hour on site when the heat index allowed at most 15 minutes of that work, or none.
  • Safe work per day: minutes of actual work if the worker keeps the rest breaks the heat requires, added up over the shift.
  • Forecast miss: a planned work hour where the real heat was a level worse than the forecast said.

What this does not prove

  • This is a test on weather records. It is not a field trial. No worker has yet been measured using Dhoop Ghadi.
  • "Actual weather" is the ERA5 reanalysis, a gridded reconstruction. A real work site can be hotter than its grid cell, especially on concrete or near a kiln.
  • The heat index is estimated from forecast variables, not measured with a globe thermometer. It uses the Liljegren reference model.
  • The limits are the ACGIH screening table, which is deliberately cautious. That is why even the planned days rarely reach eight hours of heavy work in an Indian summer.
  • The plan assumes the worker can choose their hours. Many cannot without their employer agreeing, which is why the plan is built to be forwarded on WhatsApp.
